DSCI 522: Data Science Workflows

Introduction

Data-rich projects can quickly grow out-of-hand and become irreproducible in the absence of deliberate effort at organization, tool choice, and process. This course will teach basic principles of sound data scientific workflows and will develop skills implementing them in appropriate state-of-the-art systems and languages (e.g., Python and R).

Course syllabus: This page

Course notes/book: https://ubc-dsci.github.io/reproducible-and-trustworthy-workflows-for-data-science

Slack Channel: https://ubc-mds.slack.com/messages/522_dsci-workflows

Learning Outcomes

By the end of the course, students are expected to be able to:

  • map a data analysis question to appropriate analysis
  • use a Git/GitHub to collaboratively work on a data analysis project
  • manage project software and environment dependencies using virtual environments and containers
  • write scripts and analysis pipelines for non-interactive data analysis
  • run literate coding documents non-interactively
  • automate data science workflows

Deliverables

This is a project-based course. You will work in randomly assigned groups of three (or four, if needed) for the project milestones. There are also individual weekly assignments that act as stepping stones to the project milestones. Finally, because collaboration is so important in data science, we will be using a team work multiplier to adjust individual grades. A combination of peer evaluation and GitHub history will be used to evaluate this.

WarningLab attendance is mandatory

Students are expected to attend 100% of their scheduled lab hours and attendance will be taken at the beginning of each lab session. Students must make formal requests for academic concession in the event that they cannot attend a lab session by filling out this academic concession form and sending it to the course instructor. Applying for academic concession does not promise the granting of academic concession. Each application will be evaluated in the context of UBC’s Academic concession policy. Students missing more than 20% of the total scheduled lab hours for a course without having received an academic concession will receive a possible maximum course grade of 50% and an “F” standing for the course on the transcript. For all other policies, please see the general MDS policies.

NoteWeekly oral check-ins

Each week during lab, a member of the teaching team will have a short oral check-in (about 5 minutes) with you. Think of it as practice for a technical interview: we will ask you to walk us through part of your project, such as what a script does, how a test works, or why you made a particular design choice. These check-ins are not graded. They are a low-stakes chance to practice explaining your technical work out loud and to get feedback and help along the way.

How to submit

The individual assignments, milestones, peer review and team work reflection are submitted on Gradescope. Where we need links (your repository, a release, an issue), enter the URLs into Gradescope directly: there is no PDF of links to make. Each assignment’s page says what it needs. The work itself is graded from GitHub, so push it before you submit.

Policies

Academic Integrity

Academic integrity is a commitment to honesty, respect, responsibility, and accountability in academic work. It is foundational to teaching and learning and is a shared value of all members of the UBC community. In a project course, it also means being transparent with your teammates and the teaching team about how your work was produced.

Academic integrity in this course includes:

  • Submitting individual assignments that reflect your own understanding and intellectual contribution.
  • Contributing your fair share to your group’s project, and making sure your commits, pull requests, and reviews honestly represent the work you did.
  • Citing any code, text, or ideas you draw on from elsewhere (classmates outside your group, documentation, Stack Overflow, blog posts, generative AI tools, etc.), following the MDS program policy.
  • Respecting the licenses of any code, data, or other material you reuse in your project.
  • Following the generative AI policy below, including disclosing AI use for every deliverable.

Use of Generative AI

This course follows the MDS program’s Academic Integrity and Use of Generative AI policy. Assessment-specific instructions always take precedence over the general guidance below.

AI assistance is allowed. Generative AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Claude, GitHub Copilot, Cursor) are a real part of modern data science workflows, and you may use them in this course to brainstorm, explain concepts, debug errors, or help write code and documentation. That said, this course is about building your own skills in version control, environments, containers, testing, and pipelines. We strongly encourage you to attempt the work yourself first, and to use AI to support your learning rather than replace it. A useful question to ask yourself is: is this tool helping me understand the problem, or is it doing the thinking I am here to learn to do?

Disclose your AI use. For every milestone and individual assignment, you must state whether generative AI was used, including when it was not. If AI was used, please share:

  • which tool(s) and model(s) you used;
  • which parts of the work they were used for, and how; and
  • the instructions you gave the tool, such as any system prompt or agent configuration file (e.g., CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md, .cursor/rules/, .github/copilot-instructions.md), or, if you did not use one, a few representative example prompts.

For group milestones, please keep this disclosure in an AI_USAGE.md file in the root of your project repository and update it as the project evolves. For individual assignments, include the disclosure with your Gradescope submission. Disclosing AI use is not penalized, and it helps us give you useful feedback.

You remain responsible for everything you submit. Whether code was written by you, a teammate, or an AI tool, everyone in the group is responsible for its correctness, quality, and licensing. You should be able to explain what your code does, why it is designed that way, and how it fits into the rest of your project. Please do not merge code you have not read and understood.

A human opens every pull request. AI agents may help you write code, but a group member must review any generated changes and open, review, and merge pull requests themselves. Please do not let an AI agent push to your repository, open pull requests, or merge them on its own. Because GitHub history is used to evaluate teamwork, it should accurately reflect who did the work.

Communicate in your own words. When writing issues, pull request descriptions, code reviews, peer reviews, and team work reflections, please use your own words. AI may help with translation or grammar editing. Much of what you learn in this course comes from these conversations with your teammates.

Weekly check-ins. As described in Deliverables, the teaching team will have a short, ungraded oral check-in with each of you during lab every week, as practice for technical interviews. We may ask you to walk us through any part of your project, including code that was written with the help of AI. If someone is not yet able to explain work they contributed, we will follow up to better understand how the work was produced and to support their learning. Where needed, this may also include steps under UBC’s academic misconduct procedures.

Protect private information. Do not share confidential, private, or sensitive information with AI tools, including personal information about yourself, your teammates, or other students (for example, peer review content or team work reflections).

If you are unsure whether a particular use of AI is appropriate, please ask the teaching team before you submit your work.

Academic misconduct

Violations of academic integrity (i.e., academic misconduct) may lead to serious consequences. For more details, please see UBC’s Academic Integrity website, the Academic Calendar Statement on Academic Misconduct, and UBC’s guidance on Generative AI.
